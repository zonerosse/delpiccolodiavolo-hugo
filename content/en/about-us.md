  <h2 class="section-title">Our story</h2>
  <div class="n-percorso n-storia"><div class="n-passo"><div class="n-cap"><h3>From Crufts to Ostellato</h3><img alt="Lothar Del Piccolo Diavolo — Staffordshire Bull Terrier" class="n-foto-cap" decoding="async" height="354" loading="lazy" src="/images/Lothar.webp" width="350"/></div><div><p>It all began in 2005, at Crufts. I was looking for a dog with strong but manageable character: the Staffy struck me as a “miniature Rottweiler”: powerful, determined and very affectionate.</p><p>Since then I've studied the standard, traveled to UK and Ireland, met experienced breeders.</p></div></div><div class="n-passo"><div class="n-cap"><h3>Why Elitebull and Lackyle</h3><img alt="Queen of California, daughter of CH. Elitebull Prospect and Lackyle Bean Croí Olc" class="n-foto-cap" decoding="async" height="533" loading="lazy" src="/images/queen-of-california-800w.webp" width="800"/></div><div><p>They are not two opposing styles, and it is not Ireland against England: they are two kennels that made the history of the breed, one before the other.</p><p><strong>Lackyle</strong> came first, and its historic dogs are found today in pedigrees across half of Europe. <strong>Elitebull</strong> is the one that weighs now, and its pedigrees carry precisely those historic Lackyle dogs. Not two alternative lines, then: the second contains the first.</p><p>I work with them because they are documented and verifiable lines that produce predictably. That is what you need when a mating is planned rather than hoped for.</p><p>There is also a reason that is not technical. Those pedigrees carry <strong>Lackyle Diabhal Óg</strong>, Irish for the little devil, who to my mind remains one of the finest dogs of the modern era, together with everything he left behind him. <strong>Del Piccolo Diavolo</strong> is the translation of his name: the kennel is called that because of him.</p></div></div></div>

  <span class="section-label">The Breeder</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Who is behind Del Piccolo Diavolo</h2>

  <p><strong>Paolo Boldrini</strong> has been breeding Staffordshire Bull Terriers since 2013 in Ostellato, in the province of Ferrara, under the name Del Piccolo Diavolo. Before the Staffies he bred Rottweilers, taking an Italian Champion and a European Champion into the ring.</p>
  <p>He works with Elitebull (England) and Lackyle (Ireland) bloodlines and <a href="/en/staffy-genetic-testing-l2hga-hc/" title="Staffy genetic testing: L2-HGA, HC (HSF4) and DM (SOD1)">tests every breeding dog for L2-HGA and HC</a>. Our dogs are also clear of DM-SOD1. With <strong>Bilquis Goddess Diabhal</strong> he won the ENCI Italian Beauty Champion title and placed fourth at the World Dog Show in Zagreb in 2024. Every <a href="/en/puppies-staffordshire-bull-terrier/" title="Staffordshire Bull Terrier puppies">Staffy puppy</a> leaves with an ENCI pedigree and with the parents' test certificates.</p>
  <p>The facts, data and choices described on these pages are his: they come from what happens at the kennel. For questions about puppies, bloodlines or genetic testing: contact me directly or <a href="/en/contact/">come and visit us in Emilia-Romagna</a>.</p>

  <span class="section-label">How I work</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">How I choose a male, and what I ask of families</h2>
  <p>The breeding females live here and I have known them since they were puppies. The male almost always comes from outside: it is the choice that weighs most on a litter, and I make it by looking at three things.</p>
  <div class="n-percorso"><div class="n-passo"><div class="n-cap"><h3>What the female lacks.</h3></div><div><p>A mating between two Staffordshire Bull Terriers is not about adding two beautiful dogs together; it is about correcting something and fixing the rest. If a female has an excellent head and movement that could be better, I look for a male who is strong on movement, not one who is as beautiful as she is.</p></div></div><div class="n-passo"><div class="n-cap"><h3>What lies behind the male.</h3></div><div><p>Looking at the dog is not enough: I look at his sire, his dam, his littermates and what he has produced if he already has offspring. A dog can be better or worse than his own line, and the second generation shows it.</p></div></div><div class="n-passo"><div class="n-cap"><h3>What the stud's owner can show.</h3></div><div><p>Genetic test reports with the microchip number on them, not promises. If a male is not tested for everything needed, or is clear only by parentage, I know it before the mating and I write it on that litter's page: anyone reading should know it as well as I do.</p></div></div></div><p>Before a dog is used for breeding I watch it for months: at home, with strangers, with other dogs, <a href="/en/staffy-children-family/" title="Staffordshire Bull Terrier with children: family life">with children</a>. A dog that is not sure of itself does not enter the programme, however handsome. That is why there are one or two litters a year, and some years none: if the right moment does not come, nothing is born that year.</p><p>Of anyone who wants a puppy I ask one thing first: to come to Ostellato in person, at least once. It is the only moment when I see how someone behaves with a real dog rather than a photograph, and when they see where the puppy grew up. I ask to talk about the dog's day: how many hours it will be alone, who is at home, what happens in summer when the family goes away. Ordinary questions, and they prevent almost every problem.</p>
