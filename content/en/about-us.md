  <span class="section-label">The breeder</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Who is behind Del Piccolo Diavolo</h2>
  <p><strong>Paolo Boldrini</strong> has bred Staffordshire Bull Terriers since 2013 in Ostellato, in the province of Ferrara, under the name Del Piccolo Diavolo. He has followed the breed since 2005, eight years before the first litter. Before that he bred Rottweilers, producing an Italian Champion and a European Champion, and he was an international judge of Gloster canaries. He has no ENCI kennel prefix: the dogs are registered in his name and come with regular ENCI pedigrees. The kennel takes its name from Lackyle Diabhal &Oacute;g, Irish for "the little devil", one of the dogs that made the history of the breed and that appears in the pedigrees of the lines he works with. There is one litter a year, occasionally two, and in some years none. Every litter born here, with parents, tests, examinations and growth photos, is told in the <a href="/en/diario-allevamento/" title="Breeding diary">breeding diary</a>.</p>
  <div class="n-percorso">
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>Lines, tests and results</h3><div><p>The kennel works with Elitebull (England) and Lackyle (Ireland) lines and <a href="/en/staffy-genetic-testing-l2hga-hc/" title="Genetic tests L2-HGA and HC">tests all breeding dogs for L2-HGA and HC</a>, or confirms them clear by parentage with the parents' reports, and also for DM-SOD1. With <strong>Bilquis Goddess Diabhal</strong> it won the title of ENCI Italian Beauty Champion and a fourth place at the World Dog Show in Zagreb in 2024. All puppies are placed with an ENCI pedigree and the certificates of their parents' tests.</p></div></div>
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>For questions</h3><div><p>The facts, data and choices told on these pages are his: they come from what happens at the kennel. For questions about puppies, bloodlines or genetic tests: <a href="/en/contact/">contact me directly</a>.</p></div></div>

  <div class="n-percorso">
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>Before the Staffies</h3><div><p>Before Staffordshire Bull Terriers I bred Rottweilers, and that kennel produced an Italian Champion and a European Champion. I bred Gloster canaries and judged them as an international judge: assessing one animal after another, and having to explain why one is worth more than another, is a school that serves with dogs more than it seems.</p><p>The eight years before the first litter went into studying the standard, travelling to England and Ireland and working out which lines I wanted to work with.</p></div></div>
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>Why Elitebull and Lackyle</h3><div><p>They are not two opposing styles, and it is not a matter of Ireland against England: they are two kennels that made the history of the breed, one before the other.</p><p>In the history of the breed <strong>Lackyle</strong> came first, and its historic dogs are found today in the pedigrees of half of Europe. <strong>Elitebull</strong> is the one that carries weight now, and its pedigrees contain precisely the historic Lackyle dogs. So not two alternative lines: the second carries the first inside it.</p><p>I work with them because they are documented and verifiable lines that breed predictably. That is what you need when you plan a mating instead of hoping.</p><p>Behind the choice of these lines there is also a reason that is not technical. In those pedigrees there is <strong>Lackyle Diabhal &Oacute;g</strong>, Irish for "the little devil", who for me remains one of the best dogs of the modern era, he and everything he left behind. <strong>Del Piccolo Diavolo</strong> is the translation of his name: the kennel is called that because of him.</p></div></div>

  <span class="section-label">Selection</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">How I choose a male</h2>

  <p>The females are here and I have known them since they were puppies. The male, almost always, comes from outside: it is the choice that weighs most on a litter, and I make it by looking at three things.</p>

  <div class="n-percorso">
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>What the female lacks.</h3><div><p>A mating between two Staffordshire Bull Terriers is not about adding up two beautiful dogs, it is about correcting something and fixing the rest. If a female has an excellent head and a movement that could be better, I look for a male that is sound in movement, not one as beautiful as she is.</p></div></div>
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>What is behind the male.</h3><div><p>Looking at the dog is not enough: <a href="/en/parents-on-site-what-it-means/" title="Parents on site: what it means">I look at the father, the mother</a>, the littermates, and what he has produced if he already has offspring. A dog can be better or worse than its own line, and in the second generation it shows.</p></div></div>
  <div class="n-passo"><h3>What the stud owner has in hand.</h3><div><p>Genetic test reports with the microchip number on them, not promises. If a male is not tested for everything that is needed, or is clear only by parentage, I know before the mating and I write it on the page of that litter: whoever reads it must be able to know it as well as I do.</p></div></div>

  <span class="section-label">How I work</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">One or two litters a year, sometimes none</h2>

  <p>Every mating is planned: the pedigrees are studied, the genetic compatibility is assessed, the right moment is waited for. If that moment does not come, nothing is born that year.</p>

  <p>Before using a Staffordshire Bull Terrier for breeding I watch it for months: how it is at home, with strangers, with other dogs, <a href="/en/staffy-children-family/" title="Staffordshire Bull Terrier and children: living together safely">with children</a>. A dog that is not sure of itself does not enter the programme, however beautiful it is.</p>

  <span class="section-label">Placement</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">What I ask of those who take a puppy</h2>

  <p>I ask those who want a puppy to come to Ostellato in person, at least once. It is the only moment in which I see how a person behaves with a real dog instead of a photo, and in which whoever comes sees where the puppy grew up.</p>

  <p>I ask to talk about what the dog's day will be like: how many hours it stays alone, who is at home, what happens in summer when the family goes away. They are simple questions, and they prevent almost all the trouble.</p>

  <p>And I always tell those who take one of our puppies one thing, which counts as a commitment: <strong>if one day you can no longer keep it, the dog comes back here</strong>. That is why I take my time choosing whom to place it with. Since 2013 it has never been needed: where those dogs ended up, and what life they lead today, I tell in the <a href="/en/staffordshire-family-life-stories/" title="Family stories">family stories</a>.</p>
